Case study 2 / AI product · Ad tech

AI search-term review, with a human in control.

An evidence-first review system for negative keyword recommendations, designed to reduce manual work without giving up specialist oversight.

RoleProduct designer · Prototype builder
AudiencePaid-search specialists
PatternsExplainability · Human approval · Audit log
01 / Context

Automation was fast. Trust was missing.

The original workflow used the Google Ads API and an LLM to cluster search terms and suggest negative keywords. Specialists could identify potential waste but did not want a model making account changes without clear evidence.

Product problem: Turn opaque suggestions into decisions that a paid-search specialist can confidently inspect, edit, approve or reject.

02 / Constraints

Bad negatives have asymmetric cost.

  • An overly broad negative could suppress a converting search theme.
  • Specialists needed the underlying terms, spend and conversion context.
  • Every action needed an understandable reason and audit trail.
  • The review flow still had to be quicker than inspecting thousands of terms manually.
03 / Decision

Evidence before action.

Rather than a flat list with one-click application, the proposed experience uses theme-level cards with supporting data, editable match types, confidence explanations and an explicit decision per recommendation.

Not selected

Opaque suggestion list with an “Apply all” button. Easy to click; hard to verify.

Selected direction

Terms and costs visible, editable suggestion, reject-with-reason and reviewable export.

04 / Prototype

Review suggestions yourself.

The demo uses clearly labeled sample terms. Approve, edit or reject proposals, try an exception state and inspect the local decision log. Nothing is connected to Google Ads.

05 / Outcome

Optimize for adoption and decision quality.

The experience is designed to measure time per review, edit rate, rejection reasons and unintended exclusions. The exact pilot improvements shown on the previous website were placeholders and are intentionally not presented as results.

Because suggestions are staged for review rather than directly applied, the operator remains accountable for account changes.

06 / Reflection

Next: better confidence calibration.

Confidence should account for recency as well as conversions. Structured rejection reasons would make recurring model failures easier to analyze. A larger validation set across both automotive and insurance accounts would test whether the taxonomy generalizes.
