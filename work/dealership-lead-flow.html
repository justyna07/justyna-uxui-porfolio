ContextThe funnel said where to look before anyone said why

I managed paid search for this dealership group as part of a 50-to-100-account automotive portfolio, so the first signal was financial: the store's cost per lead had drifted up for three months while impression share and click-through rate held steady. The problem was after the click.

GA4 funnel exploration on the "Get a Quote" page told the story in an afternoon. X% of mobile sessions that viewed the form started it; Y% of starters submitted; the largest single drop was at the email field, the second at "Vehicle of Interest". Desktop completed at roughly double the mobile rate on the same form.

Call-tracking data added the second half. Of calls tagged by the BDC, N% were "just researching" or wrong-department, and the median qualified call came from a visitor who had searched a model plus "price" or "lease". The form wasn't capturing intent, so the BDC couldn't prioritise.

Five remote usability sessions (Maze, mobile, recruited from the store's email list) explained the numbers. Nobody objected to giving a phone number; they objected to giving it before they knew what they'd get. Three of five read the consent text and hesitated at "and its partners".

Problem statement

Mobile visitors arriving from high-intent paid search abandon the quote form because it asks for contact details before showing any value, and the BDC cannot tell hot leads from research traffic because the form captures no purchase timing.

Success metrics, defined before design: form completion rate (mobile), qualified-call rate as tagged by BDC, cost per qualified lead.

ConstraintA vendor platform, compliance language, and a BDC with a script

  • Vendor website platform. The dealer's site runs on a third-party automotive CMS; custom forms had to be built as an embedded component, with the vendor's developer doing the build. Anything I designed had to be specified precisely enough to be built without a meeting.
  • Compliance. TCPA consent text was non-negotiable, but the wording and placement were mine to propose, with the dealer's counsel reviewing.
  • The BDC. Business development reps call every lead within a set window from a script. A form that captured timing meant a new routing rule and a script change, which is operational work the BDC manager had to agree to.
  • Attribution. Every change had to keep Google Ads conversion tracking intact. Breaking the conversion action mid-test would invalidate the measurement.

DecisionAsk what they want and when first; ask for contact last; offer a call at every step

I designed three options and tested them as clickable prototypes with five new participants.

OptionWhat it didWhy rejected or chosen
A. Trim the formSame single page, four fields instead of seven, consent rewrittenRejected. Completion improved in testing but still asked for contact first; captured no timing for the BDC.
B. Stepped form, qualify firstThree steps: vehicle, timing, mobile number. Price delivered by text. Click-to-call offered on step one and on confirmation.Chosen. Highest completion in testing; timing answer gives the BDC a routing signal; a call path exists before and after the form.
C. Call-onlyReplace the form with a tap-to-call hero and hoursRejected. After-hours traffic (a third of mobile sessions) would have had nowhere to go.
Decision. Option B. The order of questions is the design: what you want, when you're buying, where to send the price. The phone number is the last thing asked and the first thing explained.

Design details that mattered

  • Chips instead of a dropdown for vehicle and timing: one tap, no keyboard, and the answer is visible for the BDC.
  • "Text me my price" instead of "Submit": the button says what happens next.
  • Consent rewritten to say one text, named salesperson, no partner sharing. Counsel approved on the first pass once "partners" was removed, which the dealer confirmed was never used.
  • Confirmation names the rep and the response window, then offers a call for people who don't want to wait.
  • Inline errors per step that say what to do, so a missed field never clears the form.

Handoff

The vendor developer received the Figma file in Dev Mode, a one-page spec with every state, and a tracking plan: form_view, form_step, form_field, form_error, generate_lead (with vehicle, timing, lead_quality) and click_to_call (with placement). GA4 custom dimensions and the Google Ads conversion action were updated so the existing conversion kept firing and a new "qualified lead" conversion could be built on lead_quality.

Trade-offFewer fields meant more work for the BDC, and we accepted it

Dropping email and last name meant the BDC had to collect them on the call. The BDC manager pushed back: the old form did their data entry for them. We agreed a two-week trial with a script change ("I'll text you the price now; what email should I send the full quote to?") and a check on CRM completeness afterwards.

Engineering pushbackThe vendor developer flagged that the stepped form couldn't use the platform's native lead router. We kept the native router for the final submit and passed timing as a hidden field, so routing logic lived in the CRM where the BDC could change it, not in the form.

The other trade-off was speed. Option A could have shipped in a week. Option B took six, because it needed a script change, a CRM field and counsel review. I chose the slower option because it was the only one that fixed both problems the data showed.

The stepped pattern became the template for N other stores in the group, with the vehicle chips swapped per brand. The lead_quality conversion is now a bid signal in the Performance Max campaigns, which is the loop closing: the design feeds the bidding that buys the next visitor.

ReflectionWhat I'd do differently

  • I tested the prototype with five people from the store's email list, which skews to past customers. Next time I'd recruit from a panel to get true first-time shoppers.
  • The "just researching" chip is honest, but it may have invited cold leads to self-select out of the form entirely. I'd A/B test a softer label.
  • I should have instrumented form_field on the old form before the redesign so the before data was step-level, not page-level. The baseline is weaker than it should be.
